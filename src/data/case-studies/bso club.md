## Overview

**BSO Club is a Dubai-based real-estate platform that connects brokers, landlords, tenants, and service providers in one place.** At the time, the app was still in its early stages and mainly focused on brokers, with features for advertising, viewings, and offers. The product was already working, but it did not have a clear design system or consistent visual direction. The goal was to improve the overall experience and create a design foundation that could grow alongside the product as new users, features, and pipelines were introduced.

**When I joined, BSO Club was still an early-stage, broker-focused application.** It had working features for advertising, viewing requests, and offers, but the interface lacked consistency and there was no clear design system to support its planned growth. As the product expanded to serve clients, the experience also needed to accommodate new services, revenue models, and transaction pipelines without becoming unnecessarily complicated.

## Design Process

The design process followed each feature from early exploration to production. <strong>Rather than treating design as a one-time handoff, every solution was researched, tested, refined, and reviewed during implementation.</strong> This helped the product evolve through continuous releases while staying consistent with user needs and business goals.

### User Research

The research initially focused on BSO Club’s internal brokerage team, who worked closely with brokers and understood their day-to-day workflows. Through stakeholder interviews and workflow reviews, I learned how brokers searched for properties, advertised units, arranged viewings, submitted offers, and managed active deals. **These conversations acted as proxy research, helping me understand the broker experience despite not having direct access to brokers during this stage.**

As the product expanded, the research also included the needs of landlords and tenants, who had less familiarity with real-estate processes and required more guidance. We classified them as clients.

### Ideation & Strategy

After gathering insights, I mapped the main user journeys and separated shared actions from role-specific needs. Early flows and prototypes were used to explore how brokers and clients could manage properties, viewings, offers, and services without creating completely separate experiences.

![BSO Club Mind Map](<../../assets/img/case-studies/bso club/bso club 12.webp>)

Many of the feature concepts came from the wider business, while my role was to translate them into clear and consistent product experiences. **The strategy was to create one flexible system that could grow with the product, even when the business rules behind it were anything but simple.**

#### Creating a Visual Language for Every Role

The company’s logo and Poppins typeface were already established, so the goal was to extend the existing brand rather than replace it. Yellow remained the primary BSO color, supported by black, white, and grey for the main interface. Each user role was then given its own accent color and imagery: blue for landlords, red for tenants, purple for brokers, and green for vendors.

![BSO Club Typography & Color Palette](<../../assets/img/case-studies/bso club/bso club 1.webp>)

These visual cues helped users quickly recognize the role and context of a screen while keeping the overall experience connected to the same BSO brand. Colors were paired with labels and role-specific imagery instead of being used as the only identifier, making the system clearer and easier to scale as new journeys were introduced.

## Features

As the broker network and property catalog grew, the same foundation was expanded to give landlords more control over their units and connect both sides within one shared experience. Follow BSO Club's evolution, from solving broker needs first to opening the same ecosystem to property owners.

### A Catalog Built for Opportunity

![BSO Club's Search Catalog](<../../assets/img/case-studies/bso club/bso club 2.webp>)

**The Search Catalog gives brokers access to three types of properties: BSO Units, Landlord Units, and Broker Units. Each type is clearly labelled because the ownership, commission agreement, and transaction process are different.** BSO Units are managed directly by the company and allow brokers to retain their full commission. Landlord Units are uploaded by property owners using the client side of the app, while Broker Units allow other brokers to contribute their listings and collaborate through agreed commission splits.

### Moving a Deal Forward

![BSO Club Advertise, Book a View, and Send an Offer Feature](<../../assets/img/case-studies/bso club/bso club 4.webp>)

After finding a suitable property, brokers can advertise it, arrange a viewing, or submit an offer. Each action begins its own pipeline, allowing users to follow requests, respond to updates, and understand what needs to happen next.

Notifications, task manager, and status updates bring the process into one place. **The aim was to reduce the need to manage deals across several calls, messages, spreadsheets, and the occasional piece of information that mysteriously disappears.**

### Keeping Track of Updates and Tasks

<video class="case-study__content-video" src="/img/case-studies/bso-club/bso club 5.webm" aria-label="Notifications and Task Manager in BSO Club" autoplay muted loop playsinline preload="metadata"></video>

**The Notifications and Task Manager tab combines two related views in one place.** Notifications surface updates from advertisements, viewings, and offers, while the Task Manager helps users identify which requests or actions still require their attention. Together, the two views show users what has changed and what they need to do next.

The same feature is available to clients, but the information is filtered according to the services they have ordered. The service name appears first, followed only by the activities that apply to that journey. For example, advertisements, viewing requests, and offers are displayed under the Finding a Tenant service, while unrelated updates are kept out of view. This gives clients a simpler experience without removing the functionality available on the broker side.

### Contributing to the Network

![Unit Creation in BSO Club](<../../assets/img/case-studies/bso club/bso club 6.webp>)

**Brokers are not limited to properties already available in the catalog. They can upload their own units, add the required property information, and make them available to other registered brokers.** Uploaded properties can also be featured for greater visibility. This allows brokers to reach a more relevant professional audience while helping BSO Club build a larger and more useful property network.

### Opening the Platform to Property Owners

<video class="case-study__content-video" src="/img/case-studies/bso-club/bso club 7.webm" aria-label="Client Account in BSO Club" autoplay muted loop playsinline preload="metadata"></video>

As the product evolved, BSO Club expanded beyond brokers and introduced a client experience for landlords and tenants. **This allowed property owners who preferred a more hands-on approach to manage their units and order services without signing a full property-management contract.**

The experience was also designed for users who held more than one role. A broker who owned or managed a property could create a client account and switch between the broker and client sides when needed. **This role-switching system gave users the flexibility to manage professional deals and personal properties without maintaining separate accounts.**

**From the client side, users can upload properties and request services such as Finding a Tenant**. Once published, the unit becomes available to registered brokers, connecting property owners with available stock to brokers working with active clients.

### Managing the Property Journey

![BSO Club's Finding a Tenant Interfaces in Client Module](<../../assets/img/case-studies/bso club/bso club 10.webp>)

**The journey begins when a client orders the Finding a Tenant service and publishes their property to the BSO Club Search Catalog.** Registered brokers can then discover the unit and submit viewing or offer requests, which the client can review and manage directly through the app.

Viewing schedules, negotiations, communication, offers, and digital documents are brought together within one trackable journey. **This gives clients greater control while connecting them to BSO’s broker network, and gives brokers a clearer way to work with property owners without relying on scattered communication.**

### Connecting Digital Requests to Real Operations

<video class="case-study__content-video" src="/img/case-studies/bso-club/bso club 9.webm" aria-label="BSO Club's Finding a Tenant interfaces in the Client Module" autoplay muted loop playsinline preload="metadata"></video>

**Not every property service had a complete digital pipeline therefore other services are still handled by the internal team.** Users will be thoroughly informed via a Walkthrough screen before submitting a request. **User information will be then sent to BSO’s CRM so the sales or operations team could continue the process offline.** This created a practical bridge between the app and the existing business while leaving room for those journeys to become more automated in the future.

## Revenue Design

BSO Club was intended to become more than a digital service platform; it also needed to create an additional source of revenue for the company. Most of the commercial concepts were developed by the wider business team, while my role was to translate them into clear product flows and interfaces.

### Bricks

![BSO Club Bricks](<../../assets/img/case-studies/bso club/bso club 13.webp>)

Bricks were introduced as in-app credits that brokers could purchase with real money. **Think of Bricks as the digital currency of BSO Club, designed specifically to unlock premium marketing features.** Bricks give users the ability to strategically showcase their units, increasing the visibility and success of your listings. The experience needed to make the relationship between money, Bricks, and each paid action easy to understand.

### Broker Subscriptions Plans

![BSO Club Subscription Plans](<../../assets/img/case-studies/bso club/bso club 14.webp>)

**A premium subscription gave brokers access to additional benefits, including unlimited advertisements, profile customization, extra Bricks, and more.** The subscription experience was designed to communicate the value of upgrading without making the free experience feel deliberately limited or unusable.

Premium features were introduced at relevant moments in the journey, allowing users to understand their value in context. After all, an upgrade is easier to consider when users know what it will help them achieve.

### Monetize Your Network

![BSO Club Monetize Your Network](<../../assets/img/case-studies/bso club/bso club 15.webp>)

Monetize Your Network expanded the traditional referral model by bringing partner services into one place. **Third-party companies could publish their individual referral programs, while users could recommend those services and receive rewards in Bricks or account credit after a successful referral.**

Each referral included a tracker so users could follow its progress and understand when a reward became available. This created an additional earning opportunity for users while helping BSO build partnerships and introduce relevant services to its existing audience.

### Third-Party Advertising

![BSO Club Third-Party Advertising](<../../assets/img/case-studies/bso club/bso club 16.webp>)

Advertising was introduced through four placements with different levels of visibility and pricing. Refer to the image above for the order.

- The **Search Catalog** included a carousel of autoscrolling branded banners.
- The **Explore page** supported a large static placement, placed in the first fold of the screen.
- Smaller text-based placements appeared within **advertisement, viewing, and offer-request pages**.
- **Inbox advertisements** were the most prominent because they appeared like messages and could trigger notifications.

These were kept more subtle because users were already completing an important task. The aim was to make advertising visible enough to create value, but not so disruptive that the app started feeling like one long commercial break.


## Results

During my one year and eight months on the project, BSO Club evolved from an early broker-focused product into a multi-role real-estate ecosystem for brokers, landlords, and tenants.

**The experience expanded beyond its core advertising, viewing, and offer journeys** to include features such as featured units, inbox messaging, a broker network, notifications and task management, profile customization, role switching, daily streaks, achievements, and badges. This continued expansion was supported by three property pipelines, multiple monetization models, and a reusable design system that kept new features consistent across production releases.

## Conclusion

Working on BSO Club taught me that designing a growing product involves much more than creating polished screens. **I learned how to balance user needs, technical limitations, visual consistency, and commercial goals while the product continued to evolve.** These are the lessons that shaped how I now approach product design:

<ol class="case-study__numbered-list">
    <li>
        <div class="case-study__numbered-list-copy">
            <h3>A design system is product infrastructure</h3>
            <p>Building reusable components was not only about visual consistency. It made new features easier to design, discuss, and implement as the application expanded into new roles and services.</p>
        </div>
    </li>
    <li>
        <div class="case-study__numbered-list-copy">
            <h3>Consistency does not mean making everything identical</h3>
            <p>Brokers and clients shared familiar interaction patterns, but their goals and level of industry knowledge were different. Role-based colors, imagery, content, and filtered information helped create relevant experiences without turning them into completely separate products.</p>
        </div>
    </li>
    <li>
        <div class="case-study__numbered-list-copy">
            <h3>Product design includes business thinking</h3>
            <p>I did not originate every feature or revenue model. My contribution was understanding the intention behind each idea and turning it into something people could actually use. A good business concept still needs a clear experience before it can create value.</p>
        </div>
    </li>
    <li>
        <div class="case-study__numbered-list-copy">
            <h3>A partial digital journey can still solve a problem</h3>
            <p>Some services required an offline sales or operational handoff. Connecting these requests to the CRM provided a practical solution while leaving room for future automation. A product does not need to solve everything on day one, but it should be honest about what happens after the user taps the button.</p>
        </div>
    </li>
    <li>
        <div class="case-study__numbered-list-copy">
            <h3>Collaboration should continue after handoff</h3>
            <p>Working closely with developers throughout implementation helped identify technical limitations and design inconsistencies before release. Reviewing the product in production was just as important as preparing the original screens.</p>
        </div>
    </li>
</ol>

BSO Club challenged me to design for a product that was **constantly growing in users**, features, and business goals. The experience strengthened my ability to turn complex requirements into scalable and practical solutions while collaborating closely with developers and stakeholders. **More than redesigning an app, I helped create a foundation that could continue evolving after my time on the project.**
