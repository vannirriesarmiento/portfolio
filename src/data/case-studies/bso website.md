## Overview

BSO Real Estate Management is a Dubai-based property management company and digital real estate ecosystem that helps people manage properties, access real estate services, and connect with industry professionals. **It serves four distinct audiences: landlords, tenants, brokers, and maintenance vendors.** While some of their needs overlap, each group visits the website with a different goal.

A landlord may want to find a tenant or manage an investment. A tenant may need maintenance or rental-dispute support. A broker wants access to property opportunities, while a vendor wants to join BSO’s service network.

**The previous website treated these visitors as one broad audience. Its visual design no longer reflected the direction of the company, and its structure made BSO’s growing range of services difficult to understand.** The business needed more than a visual refresh—it needed a digital experience capable of recognizing who was visiting and showing them why BSO was relevant to them.

**As BSO’s UI/UX Designer and Webflow Developer, I redesigned and developed the website from research and information architecture through to production. The result was a role-based, multilingual experience combining tailored user journeys.**

## Research & Discovery

Since direct access to customers was unavailable, I based the discovery process on internal business knowledge, an evaluation of the previous website, and research into Dubai’s property-management market.

#### Learning how the business operates

I spoke with teams across BSO to understand how the company worked beyond what was visible on the website. These conversations helped me learn about its clients, internal workflows, service processes, and how different departments interacted with landlords, tenants, brokers, and vendors. This was particularly valuable because BSO offered more than a simple list of services. The website needed to reflect real business processes, including acquiring landlords, supporting tenants, connecting brokers with opportunities, and onboarding maintenance vendors.

#### Reviewing the previous experience
I conducted a content and navigation audit of the previous website alongside a general UX assessment. I reviewed:

- How services were categorized and presented.
- Whether users could identify which services applied to them.
- How easily visitors could navigate toward relevant information.
- The clarity and hierarchy of page content.
- The consistency of calls to action.
- The visual design and responsive experience.
- Opportunities to improve performance and scalability.

The assessment showed that the website treated its audiences too broadly and did not clearly guide them toward role-specific content or actions.

#### Competitive research

I compared BSO’s website with other property-management companies operating in Dubai. I examined how these companies communicated their services, established trust, structured their navigation, and encouraged prospective customers to contact them.

The comparison revealed an opportunity for BSO to differentiate itself through a more interactive and personalized experience. Rather than presenting the same conventional service catalogue to everyone, BSO could organize the website around the visitor’s role.

#### Prioritizing the audience

Landlords were identified as the primary audience because property management was a central driver of the company’s growth. Tenant, broker, and vendor experiences remained important, but the overall hierarchy prioritized landlord services and conversion paths.

#### Mapping the experience

I created a sitemap to define how each audience would enter and navigate the website. It mapped role-specific pages, shared services, supporting content, and conversion points.

This was especially important for overlapping services such as Property Maintenance and Rental Dispute. Instead of duplicating these pages, I planned how one page could adapt its content depending on the visitor’s selected role.
